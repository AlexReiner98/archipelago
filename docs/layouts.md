# Drawings

Every picture is drawn by a drawing: code a pack ships for one shape, which the owner changes on the map, run in the browser's sandbox and answering with marks the page checks. The kernel keeps only the frame every picture has (the stage that pans and zooms, the key, the bar, a published page's still frame) and draws no shape of its own.

The canvas pack (`packs/canvas`, moved out of core with its ids on 11 Nov 2026, decision e_fgsgw8ja) ships one drawing, **Map** (`core.map`, shape `map`; the owner, 9 Nov 2026): every item where an agent placed it, with no solver (the owner, 17 Nov 2026). A view that lists its islands draws each at the corner it keeps, each item at its `cell` (`layouts/map/placed.js`); any other picture is placed plainly, each island's items in rows, until the canvas draws only placed islands. Wires are straight, box edge to box edge. Its entry and marks are the flow's (`layouts/flow/index.js`), its layout `layouts/map/layout.js`. The solver it had until then (`planarize/`, `shape/`, `relax/`) is at git tag `archive/before-focus-2026-11-17`. The house style (`core.house`, canvas's) draws every view with it; canvas's own preset, which still names an old shape, draws it too. Every map starts with canvas: the page reads its numbers off the house style, so a map without it draws nothing. A style or saved view that names a shape no pack draws (one retired below) is drawn by the first drawing the packs ship, so nothing kept stops drawing (`packs/loader/kernel/layouts.ts`). A view is always fitted into sight until the reader moves it; there is no Fit setting.

**Rails** (`core.rails`, shape `rails`; decision e_btxq2nzq) is the shipped map with a walk through it: its picture is core.map's, mark for mark (`layouts/rails/index.js` runs `layouts/flow/index.js` as `map.js`), so a page baked from a view looks as the product does. Every item drawn is a stop, read in the view's order (`walk.js`: an `order` field first, then what an item depends on before it, then the answer's order), and each item's mark says its `item`. Beside the marks its scene carries `stops` (each item's id, title, first paragraph, and the camera on it: `x`, `y`, the `span` of picture it frames, `zoom` against the whole, read off where the map put it, `places`) and `whole`. A page baked from it is walked by scrolling with `packs/reader/public/rails.js` and `rails.css` (the markup they read is in rails.js's head): wide, the map fills a screen held still and scroll only moves its camera: the stop at rest is lit, the rest dimmed, and its words stand in one callout beside its box, a dot row at the foot the walk (question e_m7tssx3y); at `rails_narrow_at` or less, a column of cards with a wire between; asked for less motion, or with no script, a plain page of titles and notes. The camera's lengths are `rails_stop_span` and `rails_stop_room`; the page's the other `rails_*` sizes.

**Time** (`core.time`, shape `time`, back from the archive on 13 Nov 2026 for the time pack's week and day: task e_7tgvuyrs) draws what a view places on its time axis: a calendar (`calendar.js`: a column per day, hours running down, a lane per person and per session, each block in the colour of what it counts toward, today marked, each day's hours in its head) or bars (`bars.js`, style `layout: "bars"`). The days drawn are the reader's to choose over the picture (`range.js`, the style's `ranges`), within the view's window; a span of `time_week_from` days or more starts on a Monday and steps a week. A view a pack ships may say what its drawing sends (below): with `add`, each hour of the first person's lane is a mark that adds a block there; with `aside`, the bands the pack answers for the days in sight are drawn beside the calendar (`aside.js`), each row's acts pressed as the person.

### What a view's drawing sends

A view a pack ships names, in its style, what its drawing may ask that pack to do (`sends`: by gesture, each an action or view of the same pack; decision e_k9qu6s4g, the owner, 13 Nov 2026). The drawing calls one with `act(name, params, { asks })`; the page (`drawingActs` in `packs/walls/page/layout-asks.js`) asks the person what `asks` names, in its own dialogs, one at a time, then calls the action as them with `part: { view, hash }`, the view's id and the hash of the drawing's code. The server (`packs/loader/kernel/view-calls.ts`, beside a part's `part-calls.ts`) lets it through only from a person, for a drawing this map draws at that hash, from a view a pack ships (or one kept on the map whose preset it is) whose shipped style names the action, and never for one a person calls alone; the change names the view and the hash. What a view sends is read from the pack as it ships it, so no change on the map widens it.

## An island's own look

An island may be drawn by code of its own (decisions e_3c3wr7jv and e_r4emvndv, the owner, 17 Nov 2026): a layout kept on the map that `draws` the island (core's link of its own, decision e_gwfwbx3t; to its island item or its focus). A layout `about` an island still means what it means everywhere: the pictures of that item are drawn by it. The islands answer names it (`islands[].look`, `packs/loader/kernel/island-look.ts`); the page puts its files beside Map's own under names of their own and writes Map's `island-looks.js` to import them (`packs/walls/page/island-looks.js`), so it runs in the drawing's worker behind the same walls. Its `index.js` exports:

```js
export function island({ island, items, links, style }, { measure, m }) {
  return { width, height, places: { [id]: [x, y] }, paint: [marks], items: { [id]: [marks] } };
}
```

Each item carries its `box` ([w, h]). Places are box middles, measured from the island's top left under its header; `paint` is drawn over its land and under what it holds; `items` draws an item as it says, about its middle. Map keeps the frame's place, the header and its toggles, ghosts and held badges, each item's link and tip, and the wires (`packs/canvas/layouts/map/looks.js`). What it leaves unplaced stands beside, by cell; a look that throws or gives no size is left out. Published pages draw islands as any other.

A look that paints (its index.js exports a `default` that draws on a canvas, as Field and Lines do) is given its island's frame, and its items are painted on a surface over the map. It draws everything on its island, its own lines and hover included (decision e_a6kbgg8c, revised by the owner, 18 Nov 2026): the links among its items are never the map's marks. It runs to rest, then hands each item's place once, and again only when that moves (a new answer, a drag let go; decision e_jfctpqza): `hand({ places: { [id]: [x, y, r] } })`, in its frame's units from the top left, `r` the radius it draws the item at. The map then ends each wire from another island on that item's edge there, and draws there the session rings, ghosts, held badges and the item's click target; the pointer coming to a target is told to the look as a move to that place, and what the look says by the pointer (`tip`) stands beside it. Until it hands, its items and the wires to them are not drawn. The raster carries the look's paint alone (`layouts/flow/painted.js`, `painted-wires.js`). A look that is also to run on a map from before this passes `places` a function as it did.

## Where a drawing comes from

A pack declares it in `pack.json`:

```json
"layouts": [
  { "id": "core.architecture", "title": "Architecture", "shape": "architecture",
    "modules": { "index.js": "draw/architecture/index.js", "layout.js": "draw/architecture/layout.js", "look.js": "draw/architecture/look.js" } }
]
```

A style whose `shape` a drawing draws is drawn by it (`packs/loader/kernel/layouts.ts`). The owner's version is a layout kept on the map (core's `layout` type) with the drawing's id as its `preset` and some of its modules in `modules`: each stands in for the pack's module of that name, so changing how a picture is laid out is changing `layout.js` on the map, live. An agent's change to `modules` waits for the human (rule `code_runs`), as `code` does.

**A drawing asks in data; the page acts as the person** (the owner, 27 Oct 2026, decision e_h7he5guk). A layout's code never hears a key and never writes. It asks for a text field (`field(key, at)`): the frame's own code (`packs/walls/page/draw.js`) draws a real one over the canvas and tells the code its value (`on('field')`), so what is typed stays in the page that drew the field. It asks for a change (`change(ops, reason)`): the page (`packs/walls/page/style-code.js`) sends the ops through `change` as the person looking, naming the layout whose code asked (`drawing`, the style answer's `coder`), and tells the code what came of it. The two sides name an item's values differently: an item in the answer carries them as `fields` (`item.fields.setup`), and an op the code sends writes them as `props` (`{ op: 'update', id, props: { setup } }`); a drawing that reads `props` off the answer finds nothing. The rules read such a change as the drawing's (`packs/rules/kernel/layout-changes.ts`, `Who.drawing`): it may create, update, archive, restore, link and unlink, and core's `drawing_reach`, `drawing_code` and `drawing_public` hold for that person what writes outside the types its layout declares (`writes`), code or what a drawing may write, or makes something public. The rest applies, as the person's own act. An agent's change to `writes` waits, as code does. Code that listens for `on('answer')` is handed the answer again as the map changes; code that does not is started again.

An agent reads how a layout's code is written from the map, not from this file: `query` with name `instructions` and `for: "guide:style_code"` (the software pack's `software.style_code`). Keep the two alike when the function's inputs change.

A query's answer carries the drawing as `style.drawing`: its id, the hash of its modules, and the modules only when the page did not say it has them (`have`).

**On the log a layout's code is files by hash**, as a pack's code is (docs/log.md section 2, "Code fields"; task e_ubdb4u7c, 14 Nov 2026): `code` is the file `index.js`, `modules` a file per module, each named by the SHA-256 of its text and its text logged only when it changed, so an edit of one module logs that module alone. Every read gives the text back whole: the style answer and the drawing's hash, the frame, look, an edit and a pull see what was written.

**A layout's code may be files** (task e_4pfjppqg, the owner, 14 Nov 2026): `code` is one text, as before, or files by path, `{ "index.js": …, "physics.js": … }`, entered at `index.js`, one importing another as `'./name.js'` (flat names ending `.js`; the field is declared `files`, checked in `packs/loader/kernel/fields.ts`). The frame links them as a pack drawing's modules are linked (`load` in `packs/walls/page/draw.js`), behind the same walls. An edit names its file (`edit.file`); a try (look's `tries`) sends the files whole; a pull lays them out at their paths, and a file new in the folder joins the code, which then is files. A one-line edit of one file logs that file alone. A string stays valid: old layouts, and agents that send one text, work as they did.

## What a drawing is

Modules, entered at `index.js`; one imports another as `'./name.js'`, and nothing else. `index.js` exports:

```js
export function picture({ answer, style, reduced }, { measure, patch, open, frame }) {
  return { scene, on(event), viewport(view), restyle(style), end() };
}
```

- `answer` is the query's answer, `style` the style's fields, `reduced` whether the reader asked for less motion.
- **A session on an item is a mark in the answer** (task e_7csnapar, decision e_v6u2tk3c): each item an open session works on carries `sessions`, one `{ run, title, name, hue, state, since, age, fresh, dormant, tokens?, cost_usd? }` per session (the runs pack's `answer.sessions_on`, `packs/runs/kernel/sessions-on.ts`). `hue` is the session's own, picked by its name among the style's `hues` as everywhere; `age` is minutes since it was last heard; `fresh` runs from 1 down to 0 between the house numbers `session_fade_from` and `session_fade_to`; `dormant` is an open session not at work and silent past `session_dormant_after`, apart from both work under way and work done. Every layout draws it its own way, and fades and marks dormant alike: the flow rings the step in the hue, one ring outside another, faded to `session_faded` and dashed while dormant (`flow/session-ring.js`), and the map rings an item a painted island's look draws where the look hands its place (`hand`, below).
- `measure(text, px, font, role)` is a text's width, as the page's canvas would give it; given one of the style's text roles (`type`: weight, style, case, track), as that role sets it.
- `patch(list)` changes what is drawn, at any time; `open(id)` opens an item's page; `frame()` resolves at the page's next frame, for what moves.
- `scene` is `{ svg, key, bar, css, size: { width, height }, start }`, or `{ message }` when there is nothing to draw. A picture whose root is HTML, not an SVG (the grid's cards), or that says it is `flat` (the time axis, drawn to the page's width), is laid out by the page: not panned or zoomed, its key below it, and brought to the mark `focus` names.
- `key` says what each mark the picture uses means, one row each: `{ glyph, label, item }`, `glyph` a small mark (an `svg` of its own, checked as every mark is, drawn in the scene's stylesheet), `label` its words, and `item`, when the row stands for an item, the id its row opens. The page draws the key in the one frame every key has: on the canvas always in sight, beside the picture in the room the bars leave (the fit leaves room for it), below a picture the page lays out, above a still one; and not at all where the style says `key: "hidden"` (core's house style says `shown`). A drawing names only what it draws: the flow a row for each shape, state and link type it uses, each link type in its colour. A drawing written before the key was data may still give `legend`, marks it lays out itself.
- The page tells a drawing how wide it is (`width`: the text's width in a published document, else its host's) and how tall its host is (`height`, 0 where the host is as tall as what it holds), so a drawing can lay out to the pane's shape (the flow leans down a narrow pane and across a wide one, by `flow_aspect_follow`; the calendar sizes its hours to fit); a drawing the page lays out says so in its entry (`flat: true`), the style answer carries that, and the page gives it its box before measuring, so it is drawn once at the size it will have; a scene that says `shaped: true` is drawn again when its pane settles into another shape (by more than `plane_reshape_ratio`, still for `plane_reshape_after` ms), and the values each type's fields may take (`fields`, by type then field) and each link's title (`relations`), for the types and links the answer holds: a drawing has no registry of its own.
- `surface()` resolves, once the scene has asked for one (`surface: { key }`), to `{ canvas, size, colours, three, resized, show }`: a canvas of the drawing's own to paint (an OffscreenCanvas, 2D or WebGL), the size of its picture, the page's colours (`page`, by the theme's names, and `lens`), three.js, a way to hear the size change, and `show()`, which sends what is painted to the page as an image, shown on a canvas laid under the picture's marks (`packs/walls/page/surface.js`). There is one camera, the page's: each frame comes with the view the drawing was last told, and while the plane moves the page moves the frame shown by how far the camera has gone since (a translate and a scale), in the same frame as the marks; a frame painted where the camera rests lies still. Where the scene asked for none, it resolves to null.
- `helpers`, where it is given, is work run side by side (task e_bsp7jhrw). A drawing whose `index.js` exports `helpers` (an object of functions) is given workers of its own code by the frame (`packs/walls/page/draw-marks.js`), started as its own worker is, so the same walls hold them, pinged with it and stopped with it; a picture is handed a channel to each that is ready, as `{ size, run(name, arg, at) }`, and `run` answers what `helpers[name](arg)` came to on helper `at`, copied both ways. Their number is the machine's cores less one, one at least and six at most.
- The picture lies on one plane (`packs/walls/page/pan-zoom.js`): no edge holds it in, zoom is only scale, and the page leaves undrawn each mark (what is directly in the `svg`, or in a bare `g` directly in it) that is out of sight, a margin round the view kept drawn (the style's `plane_*` numbers). Text too small on screen is not drawn: the frame says by how big the picture's text is on screen (`data-text`: `none`, `names` or `all`, by `plane_text_least` and `plane_more_from`), and a drawing's stylesheet says what more than a name is under `[data-text=names]` (the flow folds what a choice is and the count on a step). `viewport(view)` is told what is in view as the plane moves (`{ x, y, width, height, scale }`, in the picture's units), only the latest when it moves faster than the drawing answers, and answers with patches: a drawing with more than is worth drawing at once draws what is in view.
- `on({ name, key, at })` is told of `enter`, `leave`, `click` and `move` on a mark that asked for them, and `seen` once, when the mark is first half in sight, and answers with patches; a `click` and a `move` (at most one a frame) say where, `at`, in the picture's units; `restyle(style)` is told when the reader changes what stays lit.
- **Gestures** (decision e_qpaqnmrc, axis F). `may` in the input is what the session may do (`read`, `write`, `admin`, from `/whoami`, `packs/surface/kernel/may.ts`). `change(ops, reason)` asks the page for a change and resolves to `{ status, created, proposal, held }` or `{ error }`: the page puts it to the person first (a link's type picked from those that fit, an item's type and name) and sends it as theirs (`packs/walls/page/layout-asks.js`); a drawing may ask to make one item and to link items, nothing else. `gestures({ island(key) })` is the helper a drawing opts into: `on(key)` gives `add` for an island's mark, nothing where the session may not write, so a reader's picture has no button. A mark with `on: ['add']` and an `item` (the island's id) is a button the page answers itself, without telling the drawing (`ui/marks-add.js`): it puts a new item on that island to the person. A drawing's port runs its messages in turn, so a click told to it waits for any solve under way; nothing on the plane makes or links by dragging or double clicking (the owner, 10 Nov 2026): a link starts from an item's page. Map opts in, with an Add row in each island's legend. `gestures({ pack(key) })` gives `toggle` for a pack's mark in an island's header: a mark with `on: ['toggle']`, an `item` (the island) and a `pack` (its id) is a button the page answers the same way (`packs/walls/page/marks-toggle.js`), turning that pack off or on for that island alone (its `packs_off`), put to the person first where items would wait.

- **Ghosts.** On the canvas, the answer a drawing is given holds what is held for the owner where it would land (decided by the write pack's part on the slot `ghosts` and drawn in by `packs/surface/page/replica.js`, from the pending proposals the canvas's store keeps beside its answer, read again as the queue moves): an item or link with `pending` is not yet on the map. `pending` is `{ proposal, op, by, reason }`: `op` `create` is an item a held change would make (its `id` is not an item's: draw it with no link to a page), `link` a link it would make, `update`, `archive` or `unlink` a held change to an item or link that is there. A link the person drew that the server has not yet answered for is `{ mine: true, op: 'link' }`. To let the person decide a held change where it is drawn, a drawing gives a mark the key `held:<proposal>` (anything may follow) and `on: ['click']`: a click on it puts that change to the person in the page's own words, with Apply and Decline, through the page's `decide`. The drawing only says where; it never says what the change is or decides it. The flow draws a new item faint, a link dashed in the colour of what waits on you (the person's own dashed until its fact comes), an edit as a dashed outline on its step, and a badge on each (`flow_held_*`, `flow_dash_held`, `flow_ghost`).

## Two tiers

Every drawing has one path: the answer, the style and the pins (`placed`) go in, a model is made from them, and the marks are drawn from the model. Every drawing's module list holds all it imports, and every length is a size on the style.

A wired drawing, one with wires between its boxes, adds layers. The wired ones are Flow and Architecture. Grid, Time, Pages, Meters and the social pack's channels table (`social.table`: a row per channel per month, summed from its posts, the conversations linked `came_from` them and the hours `part_of` them, sources below) have no wires.

`test/structure.test.ts` checks all of this. Each rule keeps a list of what already breaks it, and a list may only shrink: when a number comes down, lower it in the same commit, and never raise one.

## Wired drawings

A wired drawing is built in layers. Architecture is not yet: its `layout.js` and `route.js` are more than one layer, and its entries on the lists say how far it has to come.

### The layers

Each module says its layer in its first comment (`Layer: metrics.`). A layer reads those before it and nothing after. A module that has not said so yet is on `LAYERS_OWED` in `test/wired.ts`, with what it will say; once it says it, its name comes off. The layers are graph, topology, shape, metrics and marks. Only Metrics sets a place or a wire's points. What still does so elsewhere is listed by module.

### Named lengths

A distance is a size on the style, used on both axes, with no constant in the code. `test/structure.test.ts` counts each bare number of 3 or more in a drawing that does not multiply or divide, and each `px` in a string. That catches more than lengths, and the count only goes down.

### One budget

What makes a picture look better than it must spends from one budget per picture, `flow_picture_work` (`flow/effort.js`). Work is counted, never timed, so a picture is the same on every machine and each time it is asked. `packs/canvas/map-time.test.ts` keeps a large picture inside it.

### Adding a wired drawing

1. List its modules in `pack.json`, entered at `index.js`.
2. Give each module one layer in its first comment.
3. Put its layout in `layout.js`, exporting `layout`, and name every length it uses in the house style.
4. Add it to `WIRED` in `test/wired.ts`.
5. Run `npm test -- test/structure.test.ts`. A new drawing does not go on the lists: it passes each rule, or the rule says what to fix.

## Marks and patches

A mark is `[tag, attributes, ...what is in it]`; a string is text. `key` names a mark for patches; `on` lists the events it is told of; `item` says the item it stands for, as a link to the item does, and the page lights marks by it when a term of the query is pointed at (`ui/query-light.js`), every style alike: a drawing with a surface instead hears `on({ name: "light", ids })`, with `ids` null when it ends. The page makes only the SVG and HTML tags and the attributes listed in `packs/walls/page/marks.js`; a link goes only to an item's page (`#/e/<id>`); there is no script, no event handler and no style attribute. Anything else stops the drawing, and the picture says so.

A patch names a mark by its key: `toggle` classes, `set` attributes (with `over`, in milliseconds, numbers and paths move there), `text`, `mark` to replace it, `remove`, `append`.

A drawing can say what a mark is in a tip beside it: `{ tip: { key, mark } }` shows `mark` (checked as every mark is) beside the mark with that key, and `{ tip: null }` takes it away. The drawing decides when, answering `enter`, `leave` or `click` (a tap, where there is no pointer to hover), and how it looks, in its own stylesheet; the page keeps where (`ui/layout-tip.js`): above the mark or below where there is no room, inside the picture, gone when the pointer leaves it or a tap lands elsewhere, the mark's own title set aside meanwhile. The architecture drawing gives each part and each place's name one: its name as the step shows it, and its first line.

A drawing's stylesheet (`css`) is read only inside its own picture: nested under `:where([data-drawing="…"])`, so its selectors weigh what they would alone, with the picture's paint contained. What could reach outside it (`&`, `:has`, `:root`, a leading combinator, `@import`, `url(`, a quote, a backslash, a brace that closes the wrapper) is refused.

## Marks for parts

A part (a pack's piece of the app, run walled in a worker: decision e_xeqhcums) answers with marks of its own, smaller than a drawing's: nine, and nothing richer (`packs/walls/page/part-check.js`). Each is `[mark, attributes, ...what is in it]`, the attributes left out when there are none.

| Mark | Holds | Says |
|---|---|---|
| `box` | marks | `flow` row or column, `gap` and `pad` (steps 0 to 14 of the style's spaces), `align`, `grow`, `wrap`, `look` plain, band or sunk |
| `text` | words | `look` body, title, muted, mark or code, `size` (a step 1 to 9 of the style's text), `bold`, `href` (an address on the web, opened apart); on a paint surface `x`, `y`, `anchor` |
| `list` | marks | a row each, `look` rows draws a line between them |
| `input` | nothing | `key` (needed), `value`, `hint`, `label`, `lines` (more than one is a box of lines), `fits` (as tall as its words), `kind` (text, number, url, search; a pick: date, time, check, pick with its `values` and their `names`; or item, found by name by the host, of its `types`, those it may be linked to with `link`), `look` plain or title, `focus` |
| `button` | words | `key` (needed), `look` main or quiet, `label`, `pick` (item or link: pressed, the next item clicked on the canvas is told back) |
| `paint` | paths and placed text | `width` and `height` in its own units, `start`, `pixels` |
| `path` | nothing | `d` (commands and numbers only), `look` line, fill or dash, `weight` (a step 1 to 4 of the style's borders) |
| `note` | words | the map's note dialect (`packs/kit/page/text.js`), drawn as the item page draws a note (`packs/kit/page/body.js`), folded as the style's `page.notes` says; `tick` (with a `key`) lets the person tick its checklist |
| `menu` | buttons | `label` (needed), `look` main or quiet: what is done rarely, opened in sight by the host |

Any mark may say its `key` and the `item` it stands for, any mark on the page a `tip` (what it says of itself, shown by the page's one tooltip, `packs/walls/page/tip.js`), and a box, text, button or path a `tone` (accent, muted, ok, warn, bad, closed). What is not listed is refused whole with where it was, as are a key used twice, a path off a paint surface, and more than the caps in `partLimits`. A tone, a step or a look is a class or a `var()` the style gives its value (`packs/walls/page/part-marks.css`): a part names no colour and no length.

The host draws only what the check made (`packs/walls/page/part-render.js`), and each answer whole: it keeps what is drawn by key, else by mark and place, so a field being typed in keeps its words and caret. In the field the person is in, a value the part answers with that the field already sent (an echo, perhaps behind their typing) is let be; one of the part's own (the field cleared) is set. What the person does goes back as `{ key, name, value }`: `edit` and `submit` (Enter) from an input, `pick` from one whose kind is a pick (yes or no as `'true'` or `'false'`; an item as `{ id, title, type, ways? }`), `leave` (Escape in an item input), `press`, `picked` from a pick button (the item, or null when let go) from a button (one in a menu too), `tick` from a note's checklist with `{ line, done }`; a press, a tick, a pick or an item picked lets the part send one action as the person's. A part's words not yet saved are kept by the host in the person's browser (`tools.keep(value, name)`, nothing lets them go) and handed back as `kept` when it is mounted again on the same item. A paint surface pans and zooms on the page (`packs/walls/page/part-paint.js`, on `packs/walls/page/pan-zoom.js`), never asking the part; with `pixels` a canvas of its size lies under its paths, moved with them, and is handed once to whoever hosts the part (`canvas(key, el)`), to give the part to paint on.

A part is handed data, never a library (decision e_fx62gsjb): beside the item it stands on and its query's items, every input carries `words`, the schema's words of the registry its island runs (each type's `title`, `of`, `open` and `closed` statuses, each link type's title; `packs/loader/kernel/part-words.ts`, sent with `root.parts` and by its hash once the page holds them), and `style`, the style's `numbers` and `page` settings (`packs/surface/page/part-host.js`), never a colour or a length.

## A picture that plays

A view may keep `steps`: how the picture came to be, ending on the map as it is (`packs/query/kernel/query-steps.ts`). Each changes the one before it, from nothing: `add` and `remove` (ids), `titles` and `flag` (by id), `fields` (by id, then by name), `link` and `unlink` (each `[from, to]`), `chapter` (`name`, `when`, `count`) and `lines` (`by`, `text`). A status a step gives is sent with its phase, by the type of what it is given to, so a drawing reads where a thing stood then as it reads where it stands now. The last step is the map as it is: what the steps leave in, with its own titles, fields and status now, and gone if it is retired (work that is finished stays), so the still picture is always the last frame and true of what it shows. What a step names is sent with the picture even when finished: a part that is gone is told of. A published picture keeps its steps, naming only what it shows.

Every drawing plays them, each on a stage of its own over the shared player (`play.js`): the flow, the grid and the time axis lay out everything the steps ever bring in and show each thing as a step brings it, in the accent a while, so nothing moves; the flow writes the word a step flags on a thing over its corner, gives each thing the dot of the status a step gives it then, and draws a link any step makes or takes back only while it is made (a session tied to the one step it works on). Beside a canvas, the pane a milestone opens in stands empty before the first milestone: nothing is shown before it is real. The player's head says which step is shown (`5 of 32`), with a step back, Play and a step on; a step taken by hand holds there. A view up on the canvas plays its steps too (`ui/canvas.js` names it as `played`, and what they name is asked for beside its query, not added to it): there it opens on the last step, the map as it is, and plays only when Play is pressed (`style.play` "asked"); the chapters run across the top and what was said stands on the right. A step that adds a kept view (an item of the lens type) reaches a milestone: that view's own drawing opens in a pane of its own on the right of the replay's, the sash between them for the reader to slide, and moves to each later milestone with no step in that pane's history (closed, it opens again with the next); what was said moves to the left, narrow (`story_chat`). Outside a canvas (a document) the view is drawn beside the picture instead, twice its width (`story_shown_grow`); a step back shows the milestone that step had, and a milestone reached holds a while longer (`arch_story_milestone`). The player names the view with a `view` mark, which the page draws as the view is kept (`ui/view-frame.js`, as it draws a `reader`): a drawing never opens a view itself (the owner, 2 Nov 2026, e_dnewbxxh). In a document it starts once scrolled into sight. A layout's code is handed them as `answer.steps`. The architecture drawing plays them as follows (`story.js`). Each step is laid out with the one before it as its prior: places keep their order and parts their row, a part keeps its place across, and a wire its line, unless something new needs the room. Places that only call one way are stacked, wires turn as little as they can, and no two drops run within 6 units. A step that takes something away does that first, in red, while nothing else moves; then the rest move, new parts and wires in the accent, wires drawn in, every corner square throughout. The chapters are a rail above the picture; what was said is a conversation on its right, and the story starts when that conversation is scrolled into sight. A style's `dots` colours each part by a field (`layer`: kernel, pack, server pack, store, outside, yours), and every time is the house style's (`arch_story_*`). With less motion asked for, it is the last step at once. A step's `look` (`islands`) draws the story from then on as an archipelago (`islands.js`): each part's box moves into an island on a soft arc in the order its work comes, each wire is drawn again as a curved route from what is needed to what needs it, and a done island rises; the style's `dots.islands` colours it by status, with a key of its own. A step's `look` `gantt` or `kanban` lays the parts that hold work out as rows (`boards.js`): a plan, each piece of work a bar along its row, done first, then under way, then to do; or a board, the rows lanes and each piece a card in its status's column. A bar moves into a card, and both fade as the parts become islands. A step's `look` `islands_3d` turns the same islands into a relief map, painted on a surface (`deep.js`): one tile of ground with the sea floor under clear water and each island a mountain, with a contour for each piece of work inside it, coloured from the shore up as that work is done; it comes in from straight above, where it is a map of the flat picture, and tilts down while the land rises, and stays there while the work goes on. The sea runs on past the picture into a haze. Whoever a step names as the `owner` of a piece of work (`fields`) is a boat (`boats.js`), not an island: it sails, round the land, to the island that holds the work it has under way, and back to a harbour when it has none. An island that holds work is named with how much of it is done ("Website 2/4"), and islands no wire joins (projects apart from each other) each take their own place along the arc. A step's `card` (a title and a line) is what the story ends on: in relief, once the boats are home, the conversation folds away to the right, the eye rises over the map and looks up as the sea falls away into space, and the card's words stand among the stars. Its colours are the theme's `relief_*`, its heights and angles the style's `arch_deep_*`; with no surface (a still picture drawn on the server, no WebGL) the flat islands stay. A step's `fields` may give the `status` work had then, a `quick` step goes by at a fast-forward pace (`arch_story_quick`), and the last step shows the work the map has finished, not what it retired.

The landing page's story (14 steps) is checked in `packs/core/story.test.ts`: no two wires run as one, crossings (43) and turns (452) do not grow, corners stay square in every frame of every move, a step laid out again from itself moves nothing, and the last step is the map.

## The walls

A surface does not open them: the drawing paints on a canvas of its own in its worker, and only images come to the page, checked to be images; the page's canvas takes no pointer and lies inside the picture, under its marks; what comes with an image beside it is the view it was painted at, read as numbers.

The drawing runs in a worker inside one sandboxed frame (`ui/draw.html`, `packs/walls/page/draw.js`): an origin of its own, a policy that sends nothing anywhere, no page. Each picture talks to its worker over a channel of its own. A drawing that does not answer for 12 seconds is stopped. A picture it finishes is an answer, so pictures queued one behind another are each given the twelve seconds (`test/layouts-queued.test.ts`). `test/layouts.test.ts` tries all of this against a real browser.

**The owner may take the walls down for a layout's code** (the owner, 3 Nov 2026, decision e_eezrwza2): the workspace setting `drawings_open` ("Drawings unsandboxed" on the Workspace page, off unless set). With it on, the page frames `/draw.html?open=1` with no `sandbox`, which the server sends with no policy but `frame-ancestors 'self'` (`packs/walls/kernel/page-policies.ts`), and `packs/walls/page/draw.js` runs the code in that frame's window instead of a worker: the page's own origin, as the person looking, with the network, web fonts, files (a picker, a download) and links (`open` takes an `https` address too). It is handed the canvas itself and `root`, an element of the app's page laid over the picture (`.code-panel`), for a panel of its own, which an agent driving the browser reads as it reads the page; `sandboxed` says which it is. A drawing a pack ships (marks, `packs/walls/page/layout.js`) stays walled in either way, and so does every style with the setting off. The ping that stops a silent worker does not run: the code shares the page's thread. `test/layouts-open.test.ts` runs the same code both ways in a real browser.

## A view behind a page

A view whose numbers say `page_backdrop` runs its layout's code behind a published page, in a frame fixed to the window under its words (`packs/walls/page/backdrop.js`), walled in as any layout's code is. The page tells it numbers and boxes, never itself; the code tells the page its look, never touches it.

### What the code hears
- `page`, handed first and heard again with `on('page')`: `{ visible, reducedMotion, width, height, scroll, margin, view, clear, focus }` (`BackdropPage` in `packs/walls/page/draw.d.ts`), or `{ visible: false }` while the tab is hidden.
- `clear` is a box for each block of words (`pageWords` in `packs/walls/page/style-code.js`): the nav, the title, each paragraph, list and figure, the foot, so land may come between them. A call to action (the link of a `p.action`) is its own box, the link alone, and its box is also `focus`, which a drawing may centre on.
- Boxes are page coordinates: a scroll does not move them. The page reads them once and again only when its size or its words change (a ResizeObserver, fonts, a resize, a change in `main` outside a drawn view), never on a scrolled frame: scrolling costs the page no layout.
- Code that draws `margin` above and below the window and calls `drawn(top)` after each frame is shown at that page height, in a frame as tall as the page that scrolls with the words (`packs/walls/page/draw-scroll.js`).

### What the code tells the page
- `look({ ink, light, land, water, accent })`: colours the page wears as `--backdrop-ink`, `--backdrop-light`, ... on its root, so a button can wear the palette the land is in (`var(--backdrop-ink, #12110f)`). Each is a hex colour (`#rgb`, `#rrggbb`, `#rrggbbaa`); any other name or value is dropped, by the frame and again by the page (`lookOf`). A name left out is taken away, `look(null)` takes all away, and so does the backdrop stopping. Say it when the look changes, not every frame.

## When a view has drawn

An agent sees a view the way a reader does: `query look` (and `report show` with `look: true`) has the server open the app's own page in its headless Chrome (`packs/look_draw/kernel/look-draw.ts`, `packs/look_draw/kernel/chrome.ts`), as that agent sees the map, with a look pass that reads and writes nothing (`packs/look_draw/kernel/looks.ts`). The picture is taken once the view says it has drawn, never on a timer: the page in look mode (`?look`, `ui/look.js`) hands the first `view-drawn` event the canvas raises to look_draw's part (`packs/look_draw/parts/page`, the slot `look`), which publishes `data-look` and `data-look_why` on its root as page state. A drawing raises it once its scene is mounted and any surface over it has shown its first frame; a layout's code, once its default function has returned and its worker has painted two frames since (`drew`, from `packs/walls/page/draw.js`); the query, when it found nothing or failed. A view that says nothing in 15 seconds is taken as it stands (`late`). It is drawn dark unless the look asks for `scheme: "light"`: the page and a layout's code take their theme from the system's scheme, which the server's Chrome sets for each look. The answer says whether two pictures a moment apart were the same (`settled`), and carries the picture as an MCP image.

## A view in a chat

A client that draws MCP Apps (extension `io.modelcontextprotocol/ui`, SEP-1865, stable 2026-01-26) draws a view in the chat that asked for it (`packs/mcp_apps/kernel/mcp-view.ts`). The tool `draw` alone names one `ui://pelago/view` resource, `ui/mcp-view.html`, served through MCP alone as `text/html;profile=mcp-app`. The extension ties a page to a tool, not to a call, so a view has a tool of its own: an ordinary `query` or `report` opens no page. `draw` takes what `query look` takes, refuses what could not be drawn, and answers in words; a client that does not draw Apps has those words, and nothing is drawn there.

The page is one static file and holds no data, no token and no drawing. It is told of the call by the host, and for a call of `draw` it asks, through the host and its own sign-in, for a look with `marks: true`. The server's Chrome draws the view as above, in the sandbox, and `ui/look-marks.js` makes the scene the drawing answered with into markup and its stylesheet, checked by `packs/walls/page/marks.js` as every scene is, with the values the stylesheet reads: the still frame a published page shows. So the drawing is the canvas's, run behind the same two walls, and no drawing's code reaches the host's frame, which could hold neither a worker nor a second origin. It is static: it does not pan, hover or follow the map; a press on it goes to its own size, and the frame scrolls. Marks are a canvas's: a scene with a surface or a reader, a layout's code, a list and an item's page are sent as the picture a look takes, the app's bars included. A view too large for marks (`MARKS_MOST`) is sent as a picture too. Each counts as a look.

The page's policy (a `<meta>`, whatever the host sets) lets only its own script run, by its hash, and fetches, frames and sends nothing: `connect-src 'none'`. It talks only to its parent, over postMessage.

## How fast

Measured in headless Chrome with `node scripts/layout-bench.ts <server>`, 22 Oct 2026, against the kernel's own architecture drawing before it moved (in parentheses):

| Picture | First visit | Page opened again | Drawn again | Page's thread held |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page hero, 25 parts | 60 ms (49) | 19 ms (49) | 6 ms (7) | 0 ms (all of it) |
| A system of 205 parts | 194 ms (192) | 129 ms (192) | 63 ms (121) | 63 ms (all of it) |

A first visit counts from when the page starts its first picture, with the frame started while the answer was on its way (cold, with nothing started: 85 and 218 ms). Hovering answers in 2 to 6 ms, against at once before: the lighting is the drawing's, so it is asked of the worker.

What makes it so:

- **A published picture's first frame, kept at publish.** Where a picture's drawing is the pack's own (its hash is the pack's), the server runs it once at publish and keeps the scene it opens on with the frozen query (`first`, `packs/publish/kernel/published-frames.ts`), its text measured by a width table of the house typeface (`src/text-widths.ts`). The page carries its data and hints every module it starts with (`src/published-page.ts`), so the frame is mounted with the text, and the sandbox takes over when it answers. A version changed on the map is run only in the sandbox. The drawing's code is fetched by its hash, kept as immutable. Landing page, over HTTP/2 at a 50 ms round trip (`node scripts/hero-bench.ts <page data>`): the hero is on screen with the text, at 277 ms on a first visit (660 before) and 208 ms on a repeat one (573).
- **One frame, started early.** A view starts the sandbox while its answer is on the way. The frame is drawn with no size, never hidden: a frame that is not drawn has its worker slowed about tenfold.
- **A warm worker waiting.** The frame keeps one worker started, its text measuring warmed, for the next drawing to load into.
- **Code sent once.** Drawings are kept by hash, the last three in this browser; the page names them in `have`, so the server does not send them again, and loads them before the first answer arrives.
- **Widths measured once.** The worker keeps each text's width; a drawing asks again and again as it cuts a name to fit.
- **A picture is kept, and not laid out twice** (task e_vc8nnf8g, 16 Nov 2026). A picture is its answer, style, code and room alone, so the page keeps each scene a drawing answered, named by all it was drawn from (`pictureKey` in `packs/walls/page/picture-name.js`; the answer's log position left out, which says when, not what), the last 24 and at most 32 M characters of what they were drawn from. One asked again from the same (a canvas gone back to, a view switched back, the map changed elsewhere) is mounted from what was kept, measured `drawing <id>` with `kept` and nothing in the drawing, and its drawing is not started until something is asked of it (a mark hovered or pressed, a restyle: the mark `drawing-begun` says when), so a picture given back runs no layout. Asked again of the host that shows it (an answer read again that moved nothing), it is left as it is: nothing is drawn, and the view stays where the reader left it. A scene with a surface is drawn again: its drawing paints it. What is kept is the scene as the drawing answered it, checked again as every scene is when it is mounted. `test/kept-pictures.test.ts`.
- **A picture's name costs under 2 ms** (task e_rw7wkw6x, 16 Nov 2026). The style, most of what a drawing is handed and the same picture after picture, is said once and named once per text: a text met again is found by the browser's own comparison of strings. The rest is hashed over its UTF-8 bytes four at a time. Measured `picture-named`: 0.3 to 1.3 ms on a 53-item map once warm, 3 to 10 ms on a page's first picture (against 22 to 82 ms before). `test/picture-name.test.ts` holds names unique and stable.
- **Pictures kept across reloads** (task e_7as2t6eq, 16 Nov 2026). Each scene a drawing answered is also kept in the browser's IndexedDB (`packs/walls/page/pictures-kept.js`), by the same name, apart for each person in each workspace, the 48 least lately used and 16 M characters at most; a drawing whose code changed has its pictures dropped. The names held are read when the frame starts, so a picture not held is laid out with no wait. One held is mounted with no layout, measured with `kept` and `stored`, through the same checks as every scene; failing them, it is laid out. Signed out, or signed in as another, all of it is deleted (`packs/kit/page/signed-in.js`, from the token's setter) and the page lets go of what it holds: what one person was shown is never drawn for the next. Storage refused, the page draws as without it. Pulled site map, Chrome: a reload puts Home or a canvas seen before on screen at about 0.9 s, its drawing never started (Home took 2.6 s on live).
- **A narrowing changes the picture on screen first** (task e_ws9e2acq, 16 Nov 2026). An edit the page answers itself (`packs/surface/page/narrow.js`) hands the picture shown to the canvas drawn for it, and what left the answer fades out of it at once (`packs/walls/page/narrow-picture.js`): its items, the wires whose ends touched them, a head on a wire that went, a frame left holding nothing, a label on what went. The page reads this off the marks alone (an `item`, the ends of a line), indexed while the page is idle after each picture is drawn, so it measures nothing at the edit; the drawing is started once that frame is painted. When its scene lands it is matched against those marks (`transition.js`, narrowed): what stayed moves and grows from where it was, an item whole with what it holds, one whose shape changed comes in where it is, and the wires are drawn in. What it settles on is the drawing's, from the answer alone. The fade is `motion_narrow`, a wire's reach `narrow_touch`; mark `narrow shown` measures the edit to the first changed frame (20 to 70 ms on a 53-item map, against 240 to 560 before). `test/narrow-picture.test.ts`.
- **Layout off the page's thread.** The page is held only while the marks are mounted.
